Five years ago, I thought getting promoted to Architect meant writing the most clever code in the building.

I was dead wrong.

As a Senior Engineer, your superpower is execution. You get handed a messy problem, you close tickets, and you ship clean PRs.

As an Architect, your job flips completely upside down:

Your value isn't measured by the code you write. It's measured by the bad code 40 other engineers didn't have to write because your Architecture Decision Record (ADR) was clear.

Here's what nobody tells you about the Staff+ transition:

1. You trade an IDE for communication. If you can't explain why a distributed transaction is a bad idea in plain English to a Product Manager, your technical depth doesn't matter.
2. The best architecture is the code you delete. Junior engineers add libraries. Senior engineers write custom logic. Architects remove moving parts so on-call engineers can sleep through the night.
3. Consensus beats mandate every time. You don't force technical standards by decree. You build tiny internal proofs-of-concept and let team leads pull the pattern into their codebases.

The hardest part of the jump wasn't learning distributed systems. It was letting go of my GitHub contribution graph.

To senior engineers aiming for Staff or Architect: what's the hardest part of stepping back from daily coding?
